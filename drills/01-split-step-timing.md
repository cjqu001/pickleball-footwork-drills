# FW-01: Split-Step Timing

## Overview

- ID: FW-01
- Movement skill: Split step timed to opponent contact.
- Purpose: Be balanced and ready to move as the opponent hits, so your first step goes the right way without delay.
- Experience level: All players. Useful for new players who stand flat-footed and for experienced players whose timing drifts early or late.
- Format: Solo rehearsal, then partner test.
- Equipment: Paddle and balls. A partner for the test stages.
- Court area: Transition area or kitchen line on one side. The feeder stands across the net.

## Setup

- Solo stage: Stand in ready position a step behind the kitchen line, facing the net.
- Partner stages: The feeder stands at their own kitchen line, across the net. The player stands a step or two behind their kitchen line.
- Use soft, waist-high feeds that land in front of the player.

## Steps

1. Solo rehearsal. Stand in ready position. Say "hit" out loud at your own pace. Make a small split step on each "hit" and land balanced with paddle up. Take one shuffle step left or right, then return to ready position.
2. Partner cue. The feeder says "hit" at the moment their paddle touches the ball. The player times the split step to the voice cue, then moves to the ball and catches it with the non-paddle hand.
3. Read the paddle. The feeder stops calling. The player watches the feeder's paddle and times the split step to the actual contact. The feeder mixes feeds to the left, right, and middle.
4. Add the paddle. Repeat stage 3, but the player hits a soft return to the feeder instead of catching.
5. Each rep ends when the player is back in ready position and balanced.

## Suggested practice dose

- Solo: 2 sets of 10 split steps. 30 seconds rest between sets. About 2 minutes.
- Partner: 2 sets of 10 feeds per stage. 30 seconds rest between sets. About 4 minutes per stage.
- This is a suggested practice dose. It is not proven by research.

## Coaching cues

- "Small hop, land ready."
- "Land as they hit."
- "Eyes on their paddle."

## Common mistakes

- Big jump - keep feet just off the ground. Think "unweight," not "jump."
- Landing too early and then standing still - delay the hop slightly so you land close to contact.
- Landing too late, still in the air at contact - start the hop as the feeder swings forward.
- Heels down after landing - land on the balls of the feet with knees bent.
- Stage 1 treated as a reaction test - solo rehearsal only builds the pattern. Test timing with a partner.

## Versions

- Easier: Stay at stage 2 with a voice cue. The feeder tells the player the side before the feed.
- Harder: The feeder varies feed pace and height, and sometimes fakes a swing without hitting. The player should hold the split step until real contact.

## Success check

- Partner stage 3: Count reps out of 10 where the player is balanced at contact and the first step goes toward the ball.
- Practice goal: 8 out of 10.
- The feeder judges "balanced at contact." Keep the judgment simple: both feet on the ground, knees bent, paddle up.
