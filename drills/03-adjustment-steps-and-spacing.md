# FW-03: Adjustment Steps and Spacing

## Overview

- ID: FW-03
- Movement skill: Small adjustment steps before contact.
- Purpose: Get a comfortable distance from the ball so you do not hit while crowded or fully stretched.
- Experience level: All players. Especially useful for players who often hit off the wrong foot or with a bent, jammed elbow.
- Format: Partner. Solo rehearsal is possible.
- Equipment: Paddle and balls.
- Court area: Kitchen line, or a few steps behind it.

## Setup

- The player stands in ready position a step behind their kitchen line.
- The feeder stands at their own kitchen line, across the net.
- Feeds are soft tosses or dinks that land in the player's kitchen.
- The feeder mixes three feed types:
  - At the body. The player must step away to make room.
  - Slightly wide. The player needs one small step to close the gap.
  - Short. The player needs a small step forward.
- The ball should never be so far away that a big lunge is needed.

## Steps

1. Start in ready position.
2. Split step as the feeder contacts or releases the ball.
3. Judge the distance as the ball comes.
4. Take 1 to 3 small, quick steps to set a comfortable contact distance.
5. Make contact in front of the body with a relaxed arm.
6. Hit a soft dink back to the feeder.
7. Return to ready position. The rep ends when you are set.

## Suggested practice dose

- 3 sets of 10 feeds, with the three feed types mixed.
- 30 seconds rest between sets.
- About 5 minutes total.
- Solo rehearsal: Drop a ball near your feet, take small steps to set spacing, then catch it after one bounce. 2 sets of 10.
- This is a suggested practice dose. It is not proven by research.

## Coaching cues

- "Little steps, last moment."
- "Make room, then swing."
- "Contact in front."

## Common mistakes

- Ball at the body, player leans back instead of stepping - take a small step to the side away from the ball.
- One big step instead of small ones - shorten the steps. Small steps let you fine-tune.
- Feet stop too early and the player reaches - keep the feet active until just before contact.
- Feet still moving at contact - finish the last step slightly before you swing.

## Versions

- Easier: The feeder calls the feed type ("body," "wide," "short") before each feed.
- Harder: The feeder adds medium-paced balls at the body, and the player must step aside and block a soft volley while staying behind the kitchen line.

## Success check

- Count contacts out of 10 that are neither crowded nor stretched. The feeder judges by the player's elbow: not jammed against the body and not fully locked out.
- Practice goal: 7 out of 10.
