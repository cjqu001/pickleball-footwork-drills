# FW-04: Transition Stop and Reset

## Overview

- ID: FW-04
- Movement skill: Moving forward through the transition area and stopping balanced.
- Purpose: Move toward the net in stages, stop balanced as the opponent hits, play a controlled ball, and keep moving forward only when there is time.
- Experience level: Players who can hit a soft, controlled shot from mid-court. New players can use the catch version.
- Format: Partner. Solo rehearsal is possible.
- Equipment: Paddle, balls, and 3 optional markers.
- Court area: Baseline to kitchen line on the player's side. The feeder is at their kitchen line.

## Setup

- The player starts in ready position just behind their baseline.
- Optional: Place markers at roughly three stops on the way in. One near the baseline, one about halfway between baseline and kitchen line, and one just behind the kitchen line.
- The feeder stands at their own kitchen line with a supply of balls.
- Feeds are soft, low balls that land in front of the player. The feeder adjusts depth so each ball lands near the player's current stop.

## Steps

1. Start behind the baseline in ready position.
2. Move forward toward the first stop while the feeder prepares.
3. As the feeder swings, slow down and split step. Land balanced.
4. Play a soft, controlled shot that lands in or near the feeder's kitchen.
5. If the ball you hit gives you time, move forward to the next stop. If not, stay and reset.
6. Repeat the stop, hit, and move pattern until you reach the kitchen line.
7. The rep ends when you are set in ready position behind the kitchen line.

## Suggested practice dose

- 6 full runs from baseline to kitchen line. Each run has 3 feeds.
- 30 to 45 seconds rest between runs.
- About 6 minutes total.
- Solo rehearsal: Walk then jog from baseline to kitchen line, stopping with a split step at each marker. 2 sets of 4 runs.
- This is a suggested practice dose. It is not proven by research.

## Coaching cues

- "Move when they're not hitting."
- "Stop as they swing."
- "Earn the next step."

## Common mistakes

- Running through the shot - stop and split step before contact, even if it means stopping short of the next marker.
- Stopping too late, still moving forward at contact - start slowing down when the feeder begins the swing.
- Rushing forward after a high or fast shot - stay and reset. Only advance after a ball that gives you time.
- Standing up straight on the stop - keep knees bent and paddle in front.
- Backing up after the stop - hold your ground and let the ball come to you.

## Versions

- Easier: Catch the ball after one bounce instead of hitting it. The feeder waits until the player is set before each feed.
- Harder: The feeder randomly sends some feeds deeper or at the player's feet. The player must decide whether to advance, stay, or hit a reset shot. The feeder varies timing so the player cannot predict when to stop.

## Success check

- Count timely stops out of 10. A timely stop means both feet are set and the player is balanced when the feeder contacts the ball.
- Practice goal: 7 out of 10.
